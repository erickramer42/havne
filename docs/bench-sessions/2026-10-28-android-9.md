## Findings

1. **The service and its notification (8b).** The foreground service
   started with the connect (20:10:30.9; Android: "Background started
   FGS: Allowed", the app in front). Android asked to allow
   notifications at this first adapter connect; the notification read
   "Connected to …" with "· recording" while the session was up, and
   went at the disconnect. The mock got none (8a); on this fresh
   install, opening Settings' Adapter section asked for the Nearby
   devices permission, as in Phase 6.
2. **Home for 2 minutes (8b), back at 20:13:48.7:** the link stayed up
   with values throughout, no fault, and Android never froze the app
   while the service ran. Through Phase 7 the link faulted within 5 s
   ([2026-10-28-android-8](2026-10-28-android-8.md)).
3. **The screen off: the CPU slept through part of it, and nothing
   shows the gap.** The screen was off twice:
   - 20:14:24.8 to 20:16:22.8 (118 s). The phone's log falls silent
     for stretches of 51, 15 and 10 s, and the session clock, which on
     Android doesn't count deep sleep, ran 104 s behind the wall clock
     across it: the CPU slept for about 100 s. No request went out and
     no frame came in from 20:14:25.2 until the screen came back, 120
     s by the wall clock, **18.1 s by the session clock**. On return
     the link's socket was closed and reopened in 0.1 s: "Link: adapter
     fault" at 20:16:25.6, "Link: recovered" 1.5 s later.
   - 20:16:28.5 to 20:19:02.9 (154 s): the CPU stayed awake, with
     values throughout and the session clock keeping the wall
     clock's pace.

   Live and the recording both run on the session clock, so the 100 s
   of sleep left **no gap on Live and none in the recording**: the
   owner saw none, and the values on either side of it join. The
   foreground service keeps Android from freezing the app, but not the
   CPU awake; that takes a wake lock, which the app doesn't hold.
4. **Bluetooth off and on with the screen off (8b).** Off at
   20:19:40.5, the fault at once; on at 20:19:53.2, one socket connect
   (20:19:55.0, connected 20:19:56.1), "Link: recovered" at 20:19:57.2:
   **4.0 s from Bluetooth on to values** (4.2 s in Phase 7). The CPU
   slept about 3 s of it.
5. **The session as a whole (8b):** 13,864 frames over 487.6 s, none
   dropped by the bridge; frames reached the GUI thread in median
   143 µs, at most 97 ms. In Phase 7 the GUI thread stopped in the
   background.
6. **`mock://` pauses in the background (8a).** Mock and replay run
   without the service (ADR-0029 §2), so Android treats the app as an
   ordinary background app:
   - The first run (19:50:56, 486 s): Android froze the app's process
     at 19:52:49 ("freezing 19831 org.qtproject.example.qttune") and
     thawed it at 19:58:45 when the screen came on. The recording has
     gaps of 48 s at +113 s and 226 s at +246 s, and its Delivery line
     says 347 frames were dropped at the in-flight cap, one frame
     waiting 276 s for the GUI thread.
   - The second (20:21:56, Home for 70 s, then the screen off for
     103 s): no freeze, no frame dropped, but the CPU slept: the
     session clock ended 76 s behind the wall clock. Frames waited up
     to 27 s for the GUI thread. The owner saw a gap on Live after the
     screen off.
   - The third (Home for about 10 s): no gap.

   Which of these a run meets depends on when Android freezes or
   sleeps, which it decides. The owner accepted it: mock and replay are
   desk tools, and a car's session runs on an adapter.

## After the run

- Known Issue #37 stays open for one cause, the CPU's sleep: the
  service now holds a partial wake lock while it runs (ADR-0029's
  appendix), and 8b's screen off runs again on the car.
- The session clock on Android doesn't count deep sleep, so a sleep
  leaves no gap on Live or in a recording: the data are missing
  without a mark. Whether core's host clock should count it is a
  decision of its own, for v0.3.4.
- `mock://` and `replay://` pausing in the background is accepted.
