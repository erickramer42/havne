# VEHICLE — first read-only session, 2026-10-22

**Time:** 18:01–18:17 local, after a desk check on the same laptop at 17:52.
**Vehicle:** 2021 Mazda CX-5 (KF), modified, with an aftermarket ECU
tune; CAN OBD-II, 11-bit IDs.
**Adapter:** OBDX Pro VX through the x86 surrogate.
**Laptop:** a second machine, built from `main` with both trees fresh;
`scripts/verify.sh` green. The session logs show the recorder setting
payload logging and each spawn keeping the log it replaces, so its
code includes `f590613`.
**Run sheet:** [vehicle-runsheet.md](vehicle-runsheet.md).
**Evidence:** the session logs of every connect, paths normalized, and
two screenshots, in [2026-10-22-vehicle/](2026-10-22-vehicle/).
**Record sessions** was on for every vehicle connect: each spawn's log
reads "frame logging on", so every request and reply is in it.

**Result: the run sheet's first two goals are met.** The engine ECU
answered 4,809 Mode-01 requests on `0x7E8`, 4,444 of them positively,
and the replies decode to
plausible values that track the cluster: RPM matched the tachometer
through the cold start (1,000–1,460) and at idle (850–900). Those are
the STATUS conditions for tagging v0.3.0.

## Phases

**Phase 0, ignition off.** Expected "Requests failing", as on the
bench. Observed "Link quiet", no frames. All 423 writes returned
status 0, each with a `TX_INDICATION`; no ECU answered. Something on
this car's bus acknowledges frames with the ignition off, so writes
succeed and only replies are missing: the quiet state, by design.

**Ignition on, engine off, bare URI.** Connected before Phase 1's URI
was entered. Every `obd2` PID answered except `0x5C`, whose every
request got `7F 01 12`. Values: RPM 0, speed 0, load 0 %, coolant
28 °C, IAT 31–36 °C, throttle 22.0 %, fuel 72.2 %, MAF 0.1–0.8 g/s,
control module voltage 11.05–11.09 V.

**Phase 1, listen only.** No writes, every read `ERR_BUFFER_EMPTY`
(16), quiet, no frames: as expected.

**Phase 2, one PID.** `41 0C 00 00` on every request, DLC 4, RPM 0 on
Live. The supported-PID bitmaps, each identical on every reply:

| Request | Reply | PIDs marked supported |
|---|---|---|
| `01 00` | `41 00 FE 7F A8 13` | 01–07, 0A–11, 13, 15, 1C, 1F, 20 |
| `01 20` | `41 20 A0 17 F0 11` | 21, 23, 2C, 2E–34, 3C, 40 |
| `01 40` | `41 40 FE D0 8C 81` | 41–47, 49, 4A, 4C, 51, 55, 56, 59, 60 |

That is 44 PIDs in `0x01`–`0x60`, plus `0x60` itself, so a fourth
bitmap exists (not read). Of the `obd2` set, every PID is marked
supported except `0x5C`, which predicts exactly the one signal that
stayed "—" in Phase 3. Decoded against SAE J1979 Service 01 as
tabulated in Wikipedia's "OBD-II PIDs".

**Phase 3, full scheduler, engine off (18:11–18:13).** Every signal
but Engine Oil Temp populated; Oil Temp read "waiting for data", as
the bitmap predicts. Battery Voltage 10.86 V falling to 10.73 V,
throttle 22.0 %, MAF about 0.2 g/s, coolant 28 °C.

**Phase 4, engine start and idle (from 18:13:00).** The module voltage
dipped to 8.95 V as the starter engaged, and the first non-zero RPM,
207, came 0.55 s later. The tune's cold start then held 1,400–1,460 RPM for about
20 s with throttle up to 74.9 %, MAF up to 37.8 g/s and load up to
53.7 %, before settling at 850–900 RPM, 13.35–13.49 V, MAF 5–7 g/s.
Coolant rose from 28 °C to 70 °C by 18:17. The tachometer agreed
throughout. The screenshots, taken at idle, read 865 and 863 RPM,
13.42 V, load 29.0 %, coolant 52 and 66 °C, IAT 41–42 °C, throttle
14.1 %, fuel 72.2 %.

**Phase 5, ignition cycle.** Expected "Requests failing", then
recovery within about 7 s. Observed: values dimmed and froze, and came
back as the ignition went on and the engine restarted. Replies stopped
at 18:15:15.7 and resumed at 18:15:46.8, a 31.1 s gap in which all
308 writes still returned status 0, as in Phase 0. The link went
quiet, not request-failed, so no backoff ran, and the first request
after the ECU woke was answered: the engine was cranking 0.5 s after
the first reply.

## Findings

1. **Reply shape** (prep #2, #3, #4): every reply came from `0x7E8`,
   11-bit, as the adapter's reassembled message: the 4-byte ID, then
   exactly the service data (`41 <PID>` plus one, two or four bytes,
   or `7F 01 12`). No PCI byte, no padding, no checksum. No frame
   matched no channel, so the unmatched-frame flapping case never
   arose. Only the engine ECU answers the physical requests to
   `0x7E0`.
2. **Decode** (prep #7): every value above was decoded a second time
   from the logged bytes with the J1979 formulas, independently of
   core, and matches what QtTune showed. The control module voltage
   stepping from 10.7 V to 13.4 V when the alternator came in checks
   the two-byte big-endian decode; RPM against the tachometer checks
   it again.
3. **Negative replies:** every `0x5C` request the ECU answered, 365 of
   them, drew `7F 01 12` (sub-function not supported); the bitmap marks
   the PID unsupported. No `7F 01 78` ("response pending") appeared.
4. **Statuses** (prep #6): all 5,539 writes returned status 0. Every
   read that carried a reply returned `STATUS_NOERROR`; empty reads
   returned `ERR_BUFFER_EMPTY`; `ERR_TIMEOUT` never appeared.
5. **Reply timing.** Measured in the vendor's clock from each
   request's `TX_INDICATION` to its reply: median about 4 ms, 95th
   percentile 9–10 ms in every connect, longest 83 ms. But 12–22 % of
   replies in each connect are stamped before their own request's
   indication, by
   up to 43 ms: the indication marks the vendor's write call
   returning, not the frame leaving, so it bounds the reply time only
   from above. Nine of the 3,657 scheduler replies arrived after the
   next request was written.
6. **Request cadence on this laptop.** Scheduler mode averaged 109–112 ms
   per request (most 100–103 ms, some to about 123 ms when the write
   and the drain ran past the slot), so each of the ten `obd2` signals
   refreshed about every 1.1 s. `?poll=` ran at about 229 ms per
   request, 4.4 Hz, as the bench predicted.
7. **Timestamps** (prep #5): microseconds. 5,539 indications across
   932 s fit the host clock with a slope of 0.9999993 (residuals
   −1.8 to +4.3 ms, 1st to 99th percentile), increasing across every
   connect. The clock's zero fell at 17:27:25, before the VX had been
   attached to this laptop (it refused to open at 17:49 and 17:50), so
   what starts it is unknown. Its 32-bit wrap would have come at
   18:38; the session ended at 18:17.
8. **The absent VX** refuses `PassThruOpen` with status 34, like the
   VT without its adapter.
9. **UI.** The stale dimming showed the outage as intended. Two things
   read badly on the Live chart: each trace joins its last sample
   before the outage to its first after with a straight line across
   the 31 s gap, and because each trace is scaled to its own range,
   fuel level moving one or two counts (70.98–72.94 %) fills the
   plot's height. And on the laptop's screen, 1280 × 720 at 150 %
   scaling, the Dashboard's throttle and engine-load bars and the
   Settings activity log sat below the window, with nothing to show
   that either page scrolls.

## Consequences

- v0.3.0's tag conditions in STATUS are met.
- On this car, ignition off reads as quiet, not "Requests failing":
  the bus acknowledges frames with the key off. "Requests failing"
  means nothing acknowledges at all: the adapter on no vehicle, or a
  bus that's fully asleep.
- The request engine's open values ([ADR-0015](../adr/0015-request-engine.md))
  get their first measurements: replies within about 10 ms, no
  response-pending, 44 supported PIDs to `0x60` and more beyond.
- The PID-support probe would drop `0x5C` on this car, saving one
  request in ten.
