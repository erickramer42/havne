## Findings

1. **The wake lock holds the CPU awake.** Android's wake-lock log shows
   `QtTune:session` held from the connect (20:49:37.7) to the
   disconnect, and the activity log's two clocks agree over the whole
   598.4 s session to the millisecond: the CPU never slept, where
   android-9 lost about 100 s.
2. **But with the screen off the session stopped for 305 s.** The
   screen went off at 20:50:53.9 with QtTune in front, and came back at
   20:55:55.2. From 20:50:54.4 no request went out and no frame came
   in, **304.7 s**, and nothing reported it until the screen was back:
   - the bridge's 100 ms tick, on the GUI thread, didn't run for the
     whole time: values reached Live's model up to 303.5 s late, and
     "Link: adapter fault" was logged at 20:55:57.8, 2.6 s after the
     screen came on, where the link's 5 s silence rule would have
     faulted it at about 20:51:00; "Link: recovered" 1.4 s later;
   - the app's Bluetooth link does its work on the GUI thread, so
     nothing core asked of it ran, hence no requests;
   - at 20:50:54.39 Android destroyed the app's window surface, and the
     app's process logged one line in the next 5 minutes. In android-9
     each screen-off first logged the window being hidden
     (`handleAppVisibility`); here no such line appears, so the app's
     Android main thread looks stalled too.

   The cause, found at the desk afterwards (below): Qt 6.12's Android
   plugin, handling the screen going off, destroys the window's surface
   and still delivers an expose that draws; the draw waits for Android
   to make a new surface, which it doesn't with the screen off. In
   android-9 the window was hidden before each screen-off, so nothing
   drew.

   The owner saw the fault and a gap on Live after the screen came
   on. The recording has the gap too; nothing in it is lost beyond the
   frames the car never sent.
3. **Home for 2 minutes** (back at 20:59:07): the link stayed up with
   values throughout, as in android-9.
4. **The session as a whole:** 8,545 frames over 598.4 s, none dropped
   by the bridge; frames reached the GUI thread in median 127 µs, at
   most 123 ms (a measure of frames that arrived: none did during the
   stall).
5. **A Bluetooth drop (a second connect, 21:07:59).** Off at
   21:08:05.8, the fault at once ("the port failed"); the reopens
   failed "Device is powered off" on the backoff. On at 21:08:24.7
   (19 s off); the next reopen at 21:08:27.2 connected in 4.2 s, `ATZ`
   answered in 0.94 s, "Link: recovered" at 21:08:32.9: **8.2 s from
   Bluetooth on to values**, one socket connect. Slower than android-9's
   4.0 s by where the backoff stood and the slower connect.
6. **The notification was there.** Android posted it at each connect
   (21:07:59.5) and removed it at each disconnect (20:59:36.2,
   21:08:37.9). The owner didn't see it: at low importance it shows no
   status-bar icon and sits in the shade's Silent section.
7. **Live's chart over a long session** (the owner's note): the whole
   session's history squeezed into the chart's width makes a long run
   hard to read; a sliding window of the last few minutes may suit it
   better.

## At the desk afterwards (21:17–22:31)

`mock://demo` on Live and the Dashboard, the phone on USB, the screen
turned off with QtTune in front for 15–40 s at a time.

8. **The stall comes without the car or Bluetooth.** One long and ten
   short screen-offs: the Delivery line's longest wait for the GUI
   thread was 23.1 s (p99 18.9 s), the longest screen-off 23.0 s. Core's
   worker produced its 20 frames a second throughout, with no gap in
   `frames.csv`, and Android froze nothing: the GUI thread alone stopped,
   in some screen-offs and not others. The owner saw the gaps on Live.
   Six more with a debugger (`jdb`) attached didn't stall (115 ms).
9. **Where the GUI thread waits.** No root, so no native stack from
   outside (`debuggerd` needs root; `simpleperf` can't open
   `sched:sched_switch` for another user's process). A throwaway build
   carried a watchdog: the GUI thread beat every 100 ms, and a watchdog
   thread that found it 2 s stale signaled it, its handler unwinding
   its own stack to logcat. Caught on the first stalled screen-off:
   - the GUI thread: `QGuiApplicationPrivate::setApplicationState`,
     then the plugin's synchronous flush of window events, a nested
     state change, an expose to `QQuickWindow`, and Qt Quick's threaded
     render loop waiting on its render thread (`QWaitCondition::wait`);
   - the render thread (attached to the Java runtime, so named
     `QtThread`): `QRhi::beginFrame`, `QOpenGLContext::makeCurrent`, and
     the plugin waiting on its surface condition: the wait in
     `QAndroidPlatformOpenGLWindow::eglSurface()` for a surface Android
     makes, after `applicationStateChanged` destroyed the old one.
   With the basic render loop the GUI thread itself stopped in the same
   surface wait, drawing for the same expose (51 s): the draw, not the
   render thread, is the trigger.
10. **Every screen-off delivers the trigger.** With a filter logging
    what it dropped: within about 0.4 s of each screen-off, two exposes
    arrived while the app was Inactive, which Qt counts as exposed, then
    one while Suspended. Whether the draw they start hangs depends on
    whether Android destroyed the surface first, hence some screen-offs
    and not others.
11. **No drawing while the app isn't active.** The window drops
    `Expose` and `UpdateRequest` events unless the app is Active, with
    Qt Quick's basic render loop, so every draw starts from one of
    them on the GUI thread; Qt sends an expose when the app is active
    again. 12 screen-offs, then 14 with the filter's log: the Delivery
    line's longest wait 102 ms and 126 ms, the watchdog silent, the
    filter dropping both Inactive exposes every time, every page
    redrawn on return.
