### The VX's rate once replies span frames

The VX is the faster adapter with one signal and much the slower with several packed. Its packed requests take about 100-113 ms each against about 21 ms for a single-PID request (47.4 values a second unpacked, at both 4 and 8 signals). `AT ST 19` is about 100 ms. Turning packing off helps at 4 signals (11.85 against 9.61) and costs at 8 (5.93 against 6.63). The USB2CAN listened beside one more packed 4-signal VX run on USB (623 request and reply cycles, each a 2-frame reply and one Flow Control; 2,834 frames recorded and counted by the kernel, TX 0, no error). On the bus, by the kernel's stamps: request to first reply frame median 4.83 ms (p10-p90 2.01-8.32); first to last reply frame 5.08 ms (3.54-9.17); last reply frame to next request 91.30 ms (88.59-96.14); request to next request 101.08 ms (96.30-110.98). The reply reaches Havne promptly: `pair_utc.py` puts the session's stamp 0.33 ms after the reassembled reply's last frame on the bus (n 693, p10 0.26, p90 0.41). The idle time is what follows. A 20 s `obd2` session with `ECUIO_SUR_LOG_FRAMES=1` timed the VX's prompt: one PID with a count digit (`01001`) n 48 median 4.5 ms; one PID with no digit (`0101`) n 3 median 91.2 ms; six PIDs with a digit (`01040C0D1110063`) n 158 median 110.8 ms. A pack does carry a response-count digit, and the VX in ELM mode still prints its prompt about one `AT ST 19` timeout (about 100 ms) after a multi-frame reply: it doesn't end such a reply at the digit, and Havne can send nothing until the prompt. Through the MX+ each pack got as many frames as its digit said (2026-10-31-elm327-vehicle-2). Its prompts were not timed in this session.

## ModemManager and serial ports

On a stock Ubuntu desktop ModemManager probes these ports, and `systemctl stop` doesn't hold: D-Bus activation started it again. With it running and nothing to stop it, one MX+ session (RPM, 68 s) got 23 values, with `transport hard failure`, 5 s stalls and "the port is in use by another program"; the journal's `rfcomm0/probe` windows (15:31:26 to :44, 15:31:49 to 15:32:08, 15:32:15 to :33) begin after each open and line up with each failure. That run is not in the tables.

`systemctl mask --now ModemManager` stops it (the MX+ runs of the tables ran masked). This udev rule also stops it, with ModemManager active:

```
KERNEL=="rfcomm*", ENV{ID_MM_DEVICE_IGNORE}="1"
SUBSYSTEM=="tty", ATTRS{idVendor}=="0483", ATTRS{idProduct}=="5740", ENV{ID_MM_DEVICE_IGNORE}="1"
```

With ModemManager active from 15:46:21 and the rule in place, eight sessions ran: two on `/dev/rfcomm0` (a `log` of 36 s with 1,077 values and no health line, and the asking session below) and six on `/dev/ttyACM0` (the three USB runs, the listening session and the two `&pack=0` runs). The journal has 0 ModemManager lines naming `rfcomm0` or `ttyACM0` since then. `udevadm info` shows `ID_MM_DEVICE_IGNORE=1` on both.

One settling connect, 3 s after a failed connect, timed out with "didn't answer ATZ within 5 s", so a failed connect can leave the adapter slower to recover. A running session's own reopen recovers on its backoff.
