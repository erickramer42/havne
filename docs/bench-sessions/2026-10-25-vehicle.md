## Phases

**Phase 0, ignition off.** As on the first session: every write
returned status 0, nothing answered, the link went quiet at +1.25 s.
"battery 12.2 V". The probe's bitmap went unanswered ("ECU 0x7E8: no
answer to the supported-PID query; 10 requests, none dropped"), and
identification settled with every item unanswered ("13 unanswered").

**Phase 1, listen only.** No writes, quiet, no frames.

**Phase 2, one PID.** The first `?poll=0x0C` connect got no reply in
550 requests; the next, 30 s later, was answered from its first
request (the ignition was turned on between them). `41 0C 00 00` on
every reply, DLC 4, RPM 0, at 26.6 requests a second. The three
bitmaps answered as on 2026-10-22.

**Phase 4, engine start and idle (from 17:02:49).** The control module

**Phase 5, ignition cycle.** Ignition off at about 17:07:09: the link

**The laptop's battery ran out at 17:07:44**, with the engine idling.
The session log stops mid-rotation and resumes at 18:13:32, at home,

recovered at +8.15 s, and the walk resumed: "supports 53 PIDs; 10
requests, 1 dropped: 01 5C" at +8.4 s, the identity line again at
+9.6 s. Silence dropped nothing, as designed.

## Findings

1. **The request engine's pace on a car.** Service 01: 26–28 requests
   a second, so each of the ten `obd2` signals refreshes about every
   0.4 s (the first session's fixed 100 ms schedule gave 1.1 s).
   Replies came 19 ms after their request (median; 90th percentile
   30 ms, longest 116 ms, by the session log's stamps). With the ECU
   awake, one reply in 7,196 arrived after the next request went out.
2. **Service 0x22 is slower on this ECU:** replies 79 ms after the
   request (median; 90th percentile 89 ms, longest 135 ms), so the
   scan and the text DIDs ran at about 13 requests a second.
3. **Segmented replies arrive whole.** The longest were `62 F1 88` and
   `62 F1 90`, 27 bytes each (the ECU pads F190 past the VIN's 17
   characters), then `49 0A` (23), `49 02` (20) and `49 04` (19). No
   frame was flagged truncated.
4. **The VX's clock wrapped mid-connect.** Its zero fell at 15:50:48,
   shared by every connect through 17:01, so it doesn't reset at open
   or connect; 2^32 µs later, at 17:02:23, the raw stamps in the F188
   connect dropped from about 4,295 s to about 2 s. That recording's
   session times kept rising across the wrap and stayed within 15 ms
   of the host clock. After the resume the zero had moved to about
   18:13:52, about 20 s after the laptop came back, consistent with
   the adapter having restarted; what sets the zero stays unconfirmed.
5. **Statuses:** every write with the ignition on returned 0; reads
   carried replies with `STATUS_NOERROR` and came back empty with
   `ERR_BUFFER_EMPTY`; `ERR_TIMEOUT` never appeared. After the resume:
   7, then 27, as above.
6. **The battery reading** follows the vehicle: 12.0–12.3 V with the
   engine off, 11.8 V after the engine-off phases, against the ECU's
   own 11.88–11.94 V (PID 0x42) in the same minute; 0.0 V on USB power
   at the desk.
7. **Cranking readings.** One sample of Fuel Level and of Engine Load
   read 100 % during each start's voltage dip. The ECU sent those
   values; it isn't a decode fault.
8. **Recovery after hibernation** needed nothing: the link reported
   the adapter's refusals, backed off, and a new connect worked.

## Consequences

- The request engine (ADR-0015), the probe, `uds-generic` (ADR-0020)
  and identification (ADR-0021) have vehicle evidence; their ADRs get
  dated appendices with these measurements.
- The frame log's per-row storage can be sized from evidence: the
  longest reply on this car is 27 bytes.
- The run sheet's text-DID example should come from the identity line
  of the car at hand; on this one, F188 is the only answered DID that
  isn't the VIN.
