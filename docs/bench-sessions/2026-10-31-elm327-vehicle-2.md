## The packed requests

The first lap after the walk went one PID per request, as the engine
packs a PID only after its own reply has shown its length; the first
packed request went out 2.0 s after the connect. A six-PID request and its reply, from
the wire log (the count digit `3`, a First Frame and two Consecutive
Frames):

    > 01040C0D111006 3
    < 7E8 10 0F 41 04 53 0C 0B A2
    < 7E8 21 0D 00 11 23 10 01 E9
    < 7E8 22 06 84 00 00 00 00 00
    prompt 76.0 ms after 01040C0D1110063

- **The ECU answers every PID of a pack,** each record at the length its
  own reply shows, in the order asked: 41 04 (load), 0C (rpm), 0D
  (speed), 11 (throttle), 10 (MAF), 06 (short-term trim).
- From 15 s in to the close, 112.8 s: 2,604 requests of six PIDs, 162
  of five and 162 of one; no `NO DATA`, no negative reply. Each of the
  3,068 packs over the connect got as many frames as its count digit
  said. Every five-PID request is `56 59 62 63 67`, the slow class's
  run up to 68.
- **The one-PID requests are PID 68** (intake air temperature sensors),
  and each five-PID request is the slow class's run stopping before it.
  The car answers 68 with seven data bytes (`41 68 03 57 5E 00 …`, a
  First Frame), where the Service 01 table (`service01.csv`) gives
  three, so the engine never packs it: a reply longer than J1979's
  length can't be told from a packed one. The transport's existing rule
  then asks it with no count digit ("01 68's reply spans frames"), in
  both connects.
- **Core split every packed reply:** of the 3,066 replies carrying
  several records (2,889 of six, 176 of five, one of three), none was
  left unsplit. PIDs 55 and 56 went in packs at one byte, the length
  their own replies showed (`41 55 80`). Core counts 68's 177 single
  replies as unsplit (`ecuio_session_get_unsplit_replies`): each is
  longer than the table's length and doesn't split, so it decodes whole,
  which is right for the two sensors the set defines.
- The fallback didn't run: no pack failed.

`?pack=0` ran at 34.9 requests a second where the morning's connect ran
at 26.0, through the same adapter, phone and car. This sheet doesn't
explain the difference.

## Findings

1. **The test car answers several PIDs per request** through the MX+,
   in J1979's form, every PID of every pack, so packing needs no
   fallback here.
2. **Each signal refreshes four to five times as often:** fast 0.107 s
   and slow 0.665 s, against 0.521 s and 2.577 s one PID per request.
3. **Core's split holds on the car's replies,** with PIDs 55 and 56 at
   the one byte the car sends.
4. **The car answers PID 68 with seven data bytes,** where the Service 01
   table gives three: 68 goes alone, and core counts its replies as
   unsplit. Whether the table's row is wrong is to check against a
   public J1979 table.
5. **The support line repeats** when the first pack is answered, with
   nothing new in it.
6. **The strip chart is crowded,** in traces and in its time window,
   and its time labels overlap.
