Havne 0.4.0 records sessions in core, on the app and `havne-cli` alike, and reads them back: a viewer on the Recordings page that overlays two runs, `havne-cli view` and `info` offline, and a check of a recording's frames; it finds a signal among many, and its SocketCAN transport has listened through a real CAN controller on a desk bus and on the test car. Read [DISCLAIMER.md](https://github.com/erickramer42/havne/blob/main/DISCLAIMER.md) before connecting it to a vehicle.

## Try it

**Windows**

1. Download `havne-0.4.0-windows-x64.zip` and unzip the whole folder.
2. Run `havne.exe`. The program isn't code-signed, so SmartScreen may stop it the first time: choose **More info**, then **Run anyway**.
3. In Settings, tick **Developer mode** under Display, then press the `mock://demo` preset under Adapter and **Connect**.

The zip's `README.txt` covers adapters and what each file is. `havne-cli.exe` is beside `havne.exe`: `havne-cli stream mock://demo --values --count 20` prints a simulated car's frames and values.

**Linux (x86_64, glibc 2.39 or later: Ubuntu 24.04 and newer, Debian 13, Fedora 40 and newer, Arch)**

1. Download `havne-0.4.0-linux-x86_64.AppImage`.
2. `chmod +x havne-0.4.0-linux-x86_64.AppImage`, then run it.
3. In Settings, tick **Developer mode** under Display, then press the `mock://demo` preset under Adapter and **Connect**.

It takes OpenGL, fonts and FUSE from the system; a minimal install may lack them. [docs/linux.md](https://github.com/erickramer42/havne/blob/main/docs/linux.md) says what to install, how to reach an adapter (serial permissions, Bluetooth, SocketCAN) and where Havne keeps its files.

For `havne-cli` and the core alone: `sudo apt install ./havne-core_0.4.0_amd64.deb`, or unpack `havne-core-0.4.0-linux-x86_64.tar.gz` anywhere.

**Android 12 or later (64-bit ARM)**

1. On the phone, download `havne-0.4.0-android-arm64-v8a.apk` and open it. Android asks to let your browser or file manager install apps the first time; Play Protect may offer to scan an app from outside Google Play.
2. Open Havne. In Settings, tick **Developer mode** under Display, then press the `mock://demo` preset under Adapter and **Connect**.

A later release installs over this one and keeps its settings and recordings. A Havne built by CI or from source carries another key, so uninstall it first.

## What's new since 0.3.6

**Recordings in core** ([ADR-0049](https://github.com/erickramer42/havne/blob/main/docs/adr/0049-recordings-in-core.md))
- **Every frame recorded, on a thread of its own:** the app's recordings and `havne-cli stream --record <folder>` are written by core, so a recording holds every frame the session read, the ones the Log page skips under load included, and a slow disk never holds the session up. A write that fails, a full disk among them, stops the recording and says why while the session runs on.
- **Markers:** **Mark** in the header while a session records, or a line typed into `havne-cli stream --record`.
- **For programs on the C ABI:** `ecuio/recording.h`, with a recorder attached to a session before it starts, a reader that opens a recording's folder once and answers ranges, values in force and crossings, and `ecuio_recording_check`.

**Reading a recording back**
- **The Recordings page:** the recordings saved, newest first, each opening in a viewer with its signals and a chart of the part in view: drag it, zoom with the wheel or a pinch, tap for the values at that moment. Markers and what a check of its frames found sit on the chart's time axis. On a phone the page scrolls, upright or on its side.
- **Two recordings overlaid:** another recording's signals drawn dashed beside the first's, lined up on their starts, their wall-clock starts, a marker, or a signal rising or falling through a level.
- **A stretch replayed:** `replay://<recording>?from=<s>&to=<s>` plays the part between those seconds as a session of its own, and **Replay view** puts the part in view in Settings' field; `havne-cli sim` answers from a stretch the same way.
- **Offline, in `havne-cli`:** `view` prints a recording's signals over time, the values at a moment, or several recordings on one axis; `info` checks its frames for gaps, slowed IDs, late starters, overflows, broken ISO-TP sequences, link changes and sleeps.

**Finding a signal among many** ([ADR-0050](https://github.com/erickramer42/havne/blob/main/docs/adr/0050-finding-a-signal.md))
- Live's list and the viewer's signals filter by words in the name or unit, by **Plotted only**, and by the part of the car each signal reads (Engine, Air, Fuel, Oxygen sensors, Emissions, Vehicle, Electrical, Diagnostics), with a line counting the signals shown and plotted. Each signal carries its group in the C ABI, in recordings and in `havne-cli`'s output.

**SocketCAN through a real controller** ([ADR-0040](https://github.com/erickramer42/havne/blob/main/docs/adr/0040-socketcan-requests-isotp.md))
- `havne-cli devices` and Settings show each CAN interface's controller state, its modes and, from Linux 6.0, the modes its driver offers; a session the controller can't serve is refused saying which mode it needs, never suggesting one the driver lacks.
- `havne-cli sim --desk-bus` answers as the simulated ECU on a real controller, on a desk bus with no car ([ADR-0046](https://github.com/erickramer42/havne/blob/main/docs/adr/0046-simulated-ecu.md)); on any bus it goes silent for good once another node answers on its reply ID.

**Fixes**
- A SocketCAN session that sends requests no longer drops a long reply, a VIN among them, when the computer is busy.
- A SocketCAN session counts the bus rate as proven only once a request is answered, or after a quiet wait with no error frame: an adapter can echo a frame no node acknowledged.
- A replay of a recording whose last row was cut short, as a crash or a power loss leaves it, plays the rest and says so.
- The full list is in the [CHANGELOG](https://github.com/erickramer42/havne/blob/main/CHANGELOG.md#040---2026-11-04).

**Not in this release**
- SocketCAN requests through a real controller: the controller on hand offers no one-shot mode, which a requesting session needs, so it has only listened; requests on SocketCAN are verified on Linux's virtual CAN bus.
- The viewer plots a recording's decoded values, so a `raw` recording opens with nothing to plot; `havne-cli info` still checks its frames.

## What's verified, and where

Havne's claims are tiered ([docs/testing.md](https://github.com/erickramer42/havne/blob/main/docs/testing.md)):

- **Every build** runs the full test suite on Windows, macOS and Linux in CI, plus sanitizers, fuzzing (a stateful harness of a requesting session's engine and ISO-TP among them), clang-tidy, CodeQL, the load test on Windows and Linux, and a check of the C ABI against 0.3.6's. The Android and iOS apps are cross-built there; their tests don't run on a device.
- **Against the fakes and the simulated bus:** the recorder, the reader, the check, the viewer and the filter at UNIT and in the app's and `havne-cli`'s tests, the Recordings page at a phone's size upright and on its side; recording costs the worker 31 to 47 ns a frame, and an hour of 50 signals at 10 Hz, 1.8 million rows, opens in under 0.2 s on a laptop ([docs/testing.md](https://github.com/erickramer42/havne/blob/main/docs/testing.md)).
- **On a desk bus** (no car): an Innomaker USB2CAN under SocketCAN listened to the OBDX Pro VX and sent through Linux's own tools without a bus-off ([2026-11-02](https://github.com/erickramer42/havne/blob/main/docs/bench-sessions/2026-11-02-socketcan-desk.md)), then carried the simulated ECU, which the VX read over J2534: identification, diagnostics, the inspection line and moving values ([2026-11-03](https://github.com/erickramer42/havne/blob/main/docs/bench-sessions/2026-11-03-socketcan-desk-sim.md)).
- **On the test car:** the USB2CAN listened, listen-only, beside the VX reading the car, recording through `havne-cli --record` with nothing sent and nothing dropped, and timed the VX: a segmented reply is whole on the wire 14 ms after its request, and the adapter delivers it about 52 ms after that ([2026-11-03](https://github.com/erickramer42/havne/blob/main/docs/bench-sessions/2026-11-03-socketcan-car.md)). The frames check ran over that recording and every earlier car recording.
- **On the test phone** (Android 16, development builds of this release's code): the viewer drew a dense hour at 62 charts a second, the screen's frame rate there, and its scroll, pan, pinch, tap, overlay and filter were each tried by hand.
- **Not yet run:** SocketCAN requests through a real controller; the app's recorder on a car (the car's recordings were `havne-cli`'s); the iPhone app on a device. This build itself hasn't connected to an adapter or a car.

## Verifying the downloads

`SHA256SUMS.txt` holds every download's SHA-256, the debug files' and the SBOM's. One signed build provenance from this repository's release workflow covers every download, attached as `havne-0.4.0.intoto.jsonl`. Check them with the GitHub CLI:

```
sha256sum -c --ignore-missing SHA256SUMS.txt
gh attestation verify havne-0.4.0-windows-x64.zip -R erickramer42/havne
gh attestation verify havne-0.4.0-android-arm64-v8a.apk -R erickramer42/havne
gh attestation verify havne-0.4.0-linux-x86_64.AppImage -R erickramer42/havne
gh attestation verify havne-core_0.4.0_amd64.deb -R erickramer42/havne
```

Those read GitHub's attestation store; add `--bundle havne-0.4.0.intoto.jsonl` to check against the attached file instead.

The SBOM, `havne-0.4.0.spdx.json`, is attested to the zip, the APK, the AppImage, the deb and the tarball: add `--predicate-type https://spdx.dev/Document/v2.3` to check it.

The APK is signed with Havne's release key, whose certificate SHA-256 is `fb4f415a1f6c0dc56ee54dcd454c29d2226bc293c7b4c978a9b9bc8e74c4210f` ([packaging/android/release-cert.sha256](https://github.com/erickramer42/havne/blob/main/packaging/android/release-cert.sha256)). With the Android SDK's build tools, `apksigner verify --print-certs havne-0.4.0-android-arm64-v8a.apk` prints it.

## Third-party data

Havne's core carries data from others, each under its own terms, listed in [core/THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.txt](https://github.com/erickramer42/havne/blob/main/core/THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.txt), which the Windows zip and core's packages ship, and compiled into every binary:

- Trouble-code titles: SAE J2012's generic powertrain and network codes, from [Wal33D/dtc-database](https://github.com/Wal33D/dtc-database) (MIT).
- Vehicle makes: the manufacturer codes of NHTSA's [vPIC](https://vpic.nhtsa.dot.gov/downloads/), a work of the US government.
